# Competitive Gaps to Exploit

This document is for product strategy, not for copying competitors.

## Existing categories
- Canadian education/career planning: myBlueprint, Xello
- US school/counselor CCR: SchooLinks, Naviance, Scoir
- Global/UK counseling/application: Cialfo, Unifrog
- Admissions chancing: CollegeVine, SchooLinks
- Aptitude/career matching: YouScience, Xello
- Scholarship matching: ScholarshipsCanada
- Official information: EducationPlannerBC, OUInfo

## Repeated user-friction themes from public reviews/discussions
Treat these as qualitative signals, not scientific samples:
- career/planning platforms can feel mandatory, generic, boring, or quiz-heavy
- interfaces/workflows can be confusing or clunky
- counselor/application integrations can create deadline/status uncertainty
- chancing percentages are often distrusted by students
- recommendations can ignore a student's actual subject prerequisites
- mobile experiences can be weaker than desktop
- long assessments and administrative workflows reduce engagement
- expensive consulting with high-pressure sales and generic advice destroys trust

## Design commandments
1. No required personality quiz before utility.
2. No generic "explore careers" homepage as the core product.
3. Never make a student wonder why a program is recommended.
4. No exact admission percentage in MVP.
5. Every requirement gets a source + verified date.
6. Put the next actionable decision above generic content.
7. Keep the interface student-owned, not "school homework."
8. Fast mobile-first interaction.
9. Transparent pricing.
10. No acceptance or scholarship guarantees.
11. Make corrections easy; data quality is a product feature.
12. Ask for the minimum data needed.

## Positioning
"Your Canadian university decision engine."

Alternative:
"Enter your courses once. See what is open, what is missing, and what to do next."

## Defensible wedge
The wedge is not a chatbot.
The wedge is a maintained, structured, explainable Canadian admissions rules graph plus a scenario/action engine.

AI is the interface layer, not the database.
